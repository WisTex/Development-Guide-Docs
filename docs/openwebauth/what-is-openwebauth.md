# What Is OpenWebAuth?

## Overview

OpenWebAuth is an open authentication and identity protocol designed for decentralized and federated systems.

It allows people to authenticate using an identity they already control, rather than creating separate accounts for every website and service they use.

## Why OpenWebAuth Exists

The modern web is fragmented.

People are often required to create separate accounts, passwords, profiles, and identities for every website they visit.

This creates unnecessary friction, weakens portability, and places users at the mercy of individual service providers.

OpenWebAuth was created to provide a more user-centric approach to authentication and identity.

## Core Principles

OpenWebAuth is built around several key principles:

* User-controlled identity
* Decentralization
* Interoperability
* Portability
* Open standards

These principles allow users to carry their identity between compatible systems without becoming dependent on a single provider.

## How OpenWebAuth Differs

Unlike traditional authentication systems that rely on centralized identity providers, OpenWebAuth allows authentication to occur through identities that are controlled by the user or trusted providers chosen by the user.

This approach supports federated ecosystems while reducing dependence on centralized platforms.

## Common Uses

OpenWebAuth can be used for:

* Website authentication
* Single sign-on
* Federated identity
* Community platforms
* Collaboration systems
* Content management systems
* Decentralized applications

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision of OpenWebAuth is to make portable, user-controlled identity a normal part of the web.

Rather than creating a new account everywhere they go, users should be able to authenticate using identities they already trust and control.

## Conclusion

OpenWebAuth is an open protocol for decentralized authentication and federated identity.

Its purpose is to give users greater control over their online identities while enabling interoperability across websites, services, and communities.
